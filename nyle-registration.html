What Is the NYLE? And How to Register?

New York Law Exam (NYLE): What You Need to Know

If you’re on the path to becoming a New York attorney, you’ve probably heard of the New York Law Exam, or NYLE. But what exactly is it, and what should you expect?

The NYLE is often underestimated because it is an open-book exam. In practice, however, the exam has its own format and presents a very different challenge from the bar exam itself.

This guide covers what the NYLE is, how it works, what you can use during the exam, and why preparation matters even though you are allowed to consult the course materials.

What Is the NYLE?

The New York Law Exam is an online, open-book examination that forms part of New York’s admission requirements for eligible bar applicants.

The exam has:

  • 50 multiple-choice questions
  • A two-hour time limit
  • Questions focused on New York law

You need to answer at least 30 questions correctly to pass, which is a score of 60%.

Unlike the Multistate Bar Examination (MBE), which tests general legal principles applicable across jurisdictions, the NYLE focuses on New York law and New York-specific rules.

Understanding the format is important because the exam is not simply a test of how much law you can remember. You also need to be able to find and apply the relevant rules quickly.

The NYLE Is Open Book, But There Are Restrictions

The NYLE is an open-book exam, but that does not mean you can freely search the internet while taking it.

Candidates are permitted to use the official NYLE course materials and their own notes, subject to the rules established by the New York Board of Law Examiners. You should therefore become familiar with the permitted materials before exam day.

This is one of the main reasons the NYLE can be challenging. With 50 questions to answer in two hours, there is limited time to search through the materials for every question.

The goal is not to memorize every rule. Instead, you should know where important topics are located and be able to find the relevant information quickly when you need it.

You Must Complete the NYLC First

Before taking the NYLE, candidates must complete the New York Law Course, or NYLC.

The NYLC is an online course covering New York law. It takes approximately 17 hours to complete and includes questions that must be answered as part of the course.

Completion of the NYLC is required before you can register for the NYLE.

If you are planning to take the exam, it is important to account for the NYLC when planning your preparation and registration timeline.

What Subjects Are Tested?

The NYLE covers a broad range of New York law. Topics include:

  • Civil Procedure and the CPLR
  • Evidence
  • Contracts
  • Torts
  • Criminal Law and Criminal Procedure
  • Business Relationships
  • Family Law
  • Trusts and Estates
  • Professional Responsibility

The questions are based on the material covered in the NYLC and the official NYLE course materials.

How Does the NYLE Work?

The NYLE is administered online and can be taken remotely, subject to the examination requirements and technical rules established by the New York Board of Law Examiners.

On exam day, you will access the examination platform and enter the required credentials or password at the appropriate time.

You have two hours to answer 50 questions. That gives you approximately 2.4 minutes per question.

That may sound like plenty of time for an open-book exam, but it goes quickly. Spending several minutes searching for the answer to a single question can make it difficult to finish comfortably.

What Makes the NYLE Different?

There are several things that make the NYLE different from the other components of the New York bar admission process.

1. You Need to Know Where to Find the Rules

Because you can consult the course materials, you should know how the materials are organized and where to find the relevant rules.

2. The Exam Focuses on New York Law

The NYLE is specifically designed to test knowledge of New York law. This includes New York-specific rules and procedures that may differ from the general principles tested on the MBE.

3. The Exam Is Time-Pressured

The fact that the exam is open book does not mean that you have unlimited time to research each question. With 50 questions in two hours, efficient use of the materials is essential.

4. Expect Fact Patterns

The exam does not consist only of straightforward questions asking you to identify a rule or definition. Questions can present factual scenarios and require you to determine how the relevant New York rule applies.

This is why simply reading the course materials is not necessarily enough. Practicing with questions can help you become comfortable identifying the relevant issue and locating the applicable rule quickly.

When Is the NYLE Offered?

The NYLE is generally offered three times each year. The examination schedule and registration deadlines are published by the New York Board of Law Examiners.

Because registration and eligibility requirements must be completed in advance, it is important to check the official schedule when planning your exam.

Missing a registration deadline can mean waiting for the next administration, which may affect your overall timeline for admission to the New York Bar.

Final Thoughts

The NYLE is not a traditional closed-book law exam. At the same time, treating it as an exam that requires little preparation because the materials are available can be a mistake.

The combination of New York-specific law, multiple-choice questions, factual scenarios, and a two-hour time limit makes familiarity with the materials particularly important.

A good preparation strategy should therefore focus not only on understanding the rules, but also on knowing where to find them and practicing how to apply them under time pressure.

NYLE Registration Checklist (Quick Overview)

Before registering for the NYLE, make sure you complete the following:

  • ✅ Create an account with the New York Board of Law Examiners (BOLE)
  • ✅ Complete the New York Law Course (NYLC) and pass all embedded NYLC questions before the NYLE registration deadline
  • ✅ Register for an available NYLE exam date
  • ✅ Download and prepare exam materials in advance
