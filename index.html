NYLE Explained

A quick overview of the New York Law Exam — what it is, who needs to take it, and how to prepare.

What is the NYLE?

The New York Law Exam (NYLE) is an online, open-book, multiple-choice exam required for admission to practice law in New York State. It focuses on New York-specific law and rules that are not covered on the Uniform Bar Exam (UBE), so it's typically required in addition to the bar exam itself.

Who needs to take it?

Most applicants seeking admission to the New York bar — including those who took the UBE in another jurisdiction — need to pass the NYLE before they can be admitted. Requirements can vary, so always confirm your specific situation with the New York State Board of Law Examiners.

What subjects are covered?

The exam draws on New York-specific topics such as Professional Responsibility, Civil Practice, Criminal Law & Procedure, Business Relationships, Real Property, Family Law, and Trusts, Wills & Estates. Because it's open-book, the real challenge is knowing where to find the right rule quickly — which is exactly what repeated practice builds.

How to prepare effectively

Because the NYLE is open-book, memorization matters less than familiarity: knowing how questions are phrased, where key rules live, and how to eliminate wrong answers under time pressure. That's why our practice sets are organized by subject and modeled closely on the exam's question style.

This overview is for general orientation only and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the New York State Board of Law Examiners. For official, up-to-date exam requirements, dates, and content outlines, always check nybarexam.org.
